Library

The books that argue with me.

Not a reading list but a working library. These are the books I return to when a problem stops being a design problem and starts being a thinking problem.

  • The Elements of Typographic Style

    Robert Bringhurst · 1992

    Bringhurst made me fall in love with typography. This book helped me understand the rules, why they exist, and how much care goes into setting even a few lines of text.

  • Victor Papanek: Designer for the Real World

    Alison J. Clarke · MIT Press, 2021

    Papanek questioned the brief itself. Asked to design machines selling chocolate in schools, he turned to children’s nutrition instead. That stayed with me: an important design decision is often deciding which problem is worth solving.

  • Thinking in Systems

    Donella Meadows · 2008

    This book helped me look beyond the immediate problem. What happens in one part of a system often has its causes somewhere else. I find myself coming back to that idea often.

  • The Laws of Simplicity

    John Maeda · 2006

    I love Maeda’s work, and this small book shows why. Ten laws for making things simpler, with practical guidance on organising and deciding what matters. Its writing, illustrations and layout are as considered as its ideas: content and form together.

  • Thinking, Fast and Slow

    Daniel Kahneman · 2011

    A book that made me more aware of how easily we make assumptions and how little we sometimes understand about our own decisions. It’s been useful both in research and in questioning my own conclusions.

  • Mapping Experiences

    James Kalbach · 2016

    What I like about this book is that it treats an experience as more than a sequence of screens. It brings together physical spaces, digital interactions and the processes behind them. A very practical way to understand how services actually work.
